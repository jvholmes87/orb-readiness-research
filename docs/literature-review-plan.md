# Literature Review Plan

## Purpose

This review is the highest-priority research task. Its purpose is to determine whether the proposed research gap is real before ORB finalizes a codebook, taxonomy, instrument, or doctoral-scope claim.

A practical problem observed in federal construction or supplier-development work is not, by itself, evidence of scholarly novelty.

## Priority journals

Start with:

- *Journal of Construction Engineering and Management*
- *Automation in Construction*
- *Journal of Management in Engineering*

Then expand through citation chaining and relevant systems-engineering, acquisition, operations, small-business, regulatory-compliance, and supplier-development literature.

## Core search families

Use these as controlled search families, then record synonyms and database-specific variants:

1. `rework causation`
2. `submittal review`
3. `RFI latency`
4. `construction defect taxonomy`
5. `requirements volatility`
6. `regulatory compliance cost small firms`

Also search combinations involving supplier qualification, evidence readiness, documentation burden, administrative burden, small contractors, subcontractors, federal construction, aerospace suppliers, and lower-tier supply chains.

## Citation-network lead

Peter E. D. Love and related rework literature should be used as a citation-network starting point because existing research may already explain parts of the proposed mechanism. This is a search lead, not a conclusion that the proposed gap has been resolved.

## Review questions

The review should answer:

- What constructs already exist for rework, review delay, nonconformance, administrative burden, requirements change, and qualification burden?
- What units of analysis are used—document, line item, package, RFI, submittal, project, firm, contract, or organization?
- Which causes of rejection or rework have already been categorized and validated?
- How are review latency and external waiting time measured?
- What evidence exists specifically for small firms or subcontractors?
- What evidence exists for federal work, overseas execution, foreign-national labor, or cross-organizational approval chains?
- Are there validated instruments for measuring documentation, obligation, or compliance burden?
- Has data-model enforcement of obligations been compared with primarily procedural enforcement?
- Which gaps remain after controlling for what the literature already knows?

## Search log fields

For every database/search iteration, retain:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Search ID | Stable identifier |
| Database / index | Where the search was run |
| Date | Search date |
| Exact query | Reproducible search string |
| Filters | Years, document type, language, discipline |
| Results returned | Raw result count |
| Records screened | Count reviewed at title/abstract stage |
| Records retained | Count moved to full-text review |
| Exclusion reason | Controlled reason where practical |
| Notes | Terminology or citation-network discoveries |

## Evidence table fields

For retained studies, capture at minimum:

- full citation and DOI/stable identifier
- research question
- population and context
- unit of analysis
- sample size
- construct definitions
- variables and measures
- method/design
- reliability/validity evidence
- principal findings
- limitations
- direct relevance to ORB or the federal-compliance companion study
- whether the paper supports, narrows, contradicts, or leaves open the proposed gap

## Sequence gate

The following work is deferred until this review reaches a defensible first synthesis:

- final rejection/failure codebook
- final taxonomy terminology
- validated-instrument claims
- dissertation novelty statements
- causal claims about the deployed federal compliance model

The review may result in one of three outcomes:

1. **Gap supported:** an unresolved problem remains and can be stated precisely.
2. **Gap narrowed:** substantial prior work exists, but a more specific population, mechanism, measurement, or comparison remains unresolved.
3. **Gap not established:** the proposed contribution substantially duplicates existing research and must be redesigned.

All three are acceptable research outcomes.
