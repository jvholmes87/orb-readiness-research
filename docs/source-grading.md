# Source Grading

## Purpose

The grading scheme records source authority separately from claim-verification status. A strong source may support only part of a claim. A weak source does not become verified through repetition.

## Evidence grades

| Grade | Source class | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Primary official record, direct government-office confirmation, executed instrument, or original research record | Supports a bounded factual claim within the source's scope |
| B | Official secondary source, agency summary, council summary, or institutional webpage | Supports descriptive context with date and scope qualifiers |
| C | Industry, consultancy, trade association, practitioner panel, or vendor source | Supports attributed industry statements, hypotheses, or comparison points |
| D | ORB working document, calculation, interpretation, or research hypothesis | Supports research design and clearly labeled derived analysis |
| X | Assertion with no located paper trail or evidence that does not support the stated claim | Cannot be cited as fact |

Grade A includes direct office correspondence when authenticity and provenance are retained in the restricted research record. Public output cites the responsible office and does not reproduce message text or identify an individual.

## Verification statuses

| Status | Rule |
|---|---|
| `Verified` | The evidence directly supports every material element of the bounded claim. |
| `Partially verified` | At least one material element is supported and at least one remains unsupported, unresolved, or unavailable. |
| `Not located` | The stated source or evidence was not found in the documented search locations by the check date. |
| `Superseded` | A later authoritative source corrects or replaces the earlier value or statement. |
| `Unverified` | Review is incomplete or the current evidence is insufficient. |

Verification status applies to a claim at a stated date. It does not apply permanently to a source or organization.

## Claim record requirements

Every factual claim entered in the research record should contain:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Claim ID | Stable identifier |
| Claim text | One bounded, testable statement |
| Status | Current verification status |
| Source grade | Authority class for each supporting source |
| Responsible office or publisher | Organization-level attribution |
| Source title or record description | Sufficient identification without exposing restricted content |
| Publication or confirmation date | Source date when known |
| Check date | Date the researcher evaluated the claim |
| Search locations | Databases, websites, folders, or registers reviewed |
| Scope qualifier | Population, time period, denominator, and exclusions |
| Public-use wording | Sanitized language approved for public output |
| Supersession link | Prior and replacement claim IDs when corrected |

## Rules for direct correspondence

Direct correspondence can verify what the responsible office stated. It cannot establish facts beyond the office's authority or the wording confirmed. Public citations use the office name, date, bounded subject, and restricted-record notation.

The public repository excludes message text, personal names, contact information, and characterizations of responsiveness or conduct. Independent reproduction may be limited when the evidence is not publicly accessible, and that limitation must be disclosed.

## Rules for quantitative claims

Every quantitative claim must include the value, unit, population, time period or snapshot date, source, calculation status, and relevant denominator. Derived values receive Grade D until their inputs and calculation are independently checked.

When an authoritative correction is received, the older value remains in the audit trail as `Superseded`. Current narrative uses the corrected value and explains the change when it affects interpretation.

## Search and absence language

Failure to locate a record supports only a bounded search result. Use the following construction:

> The record was not located in public records as of [date] after review of [locations].

Do not infer concealment, misconduct, nonexistence, or motive from a search result. Record the query, locations, filters, and date so the search can be repeated.

## Conflict handling

Conflicting sources remain visible until an authoritative resolution is obtained. The register records both claims, their grades, and the current handling decision. The researcher does not average incompatible values or select one without a stated basis.
