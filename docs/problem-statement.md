# Problem Statement

## Research status

This document defines a research problem and working hypothesis. ORB remains in requirements gathering. No fieldwork has been conducted, no sample exists, and no supplier-readiness outcome has been measured.

## Forecasts and supplier readiness

Economic and market forecasts can describe expected demand, investment, production, or employment. A forecast does not demonstrate that small firms in a target region can satisfy buyer-specific qualification and documentation requirements.

Supplier participation may require controlled procedures, quality records, traceable measurements, cybersecurity evidence, approved processes, financial and delivery controls, registrations, and recurring certifications. The applicable combination depends on the buyer, product, contract, risk, and supply-chain tier. ORB treats these requirements as variables to be identified rather than a universal checklist.

## Working hypothesis

ORB's working hypothesis is that documentation and qualification burden can be a binding constraint on small-firm participation in federal and aerospace supply chains even when a firm has relevant technical capability. The study will test whether firms can identify applicable requirements, produce acceptable evidence, maintain that evidence, and respond to changes at manageable effort.

Technical capability and qualification readiness are separate constructs in the proposed design. A firm may be capable of performing a process while lacking the documented controls required by a buyer. A firm may also hold formal credentials that do not match a specific procurement requirement.

## Proposed mechanism

The proposed mechanism has five stages:

1. External requirements are discovered and assigned to the firm.
2. Requirements are translated into controlled obligations and evidence criteria.
3. The firm produces or obtains the required evidence.
4. A buyer, prime contractor, registrar, or authorized reviewer adjudicates the evidence where applicable.
5. Time-limited qualifications and records are monitored for renewal.

Failure can occur at each stage. An unrecognized requirement produces no planned evidence. An ambiguous obligation produces inconsistent implementation. A valid artifact can still fail if it is routed incorrectly, expires, or cannot be traced to the governing requirement.

## Relationship to federal construction compliance

The `federal-submittal-compliance-model` documents the same mechanism in a different population. Its population is a small subcontractor performing federal construction task orders at an overseas installation. ORB shifts the unit of analysis to small manufacturers and suppliers seeking access to aerospace and advanced manufacturing supply chains.

The comparison is conceptual at this stage. No cross-population equivalence or causal transfer has been established.

## Testable questions

- Which qualification and documentation obligations apply to each participating firm and target opportunity?
- What proportion of applicable evidence is present, current, traceable, and acceptable at baseline?
- Which obligations create the greatest preparation time, external cost, dependency, or renewal burden?
- At which lifecycle stage do firms most often stop, wait, repeat work, or require referral?
- Which forms of assistance are associated with completed evidence or qualification milestones?
- Which requirements differ by buyer, contract, product, risk, and supply-chain tier?
