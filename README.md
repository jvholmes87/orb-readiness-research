# ORB Readiness Research

> **Current status:** Requirements gathering only. No fieldwork has been conducted. No entity has been formed. The project has no funding, site control, sample, or authority to certify or qualify any firm. It does not represent an incorporated organization. **Research novelty has not been established; a focused literature review now precedes instrument finalization or any doctoral-scope claim.**

## Purpose

Our Right to Build (ORB) is an independent research effort examining whether small manufacturers and suppliers can meet the qualification and documentation requirements associated with aerospace and advanced manufacturing supply chains in the New Orleans East and Michoud corridor. This repository publishes the research design, evidence rules, verification status, current limitations, and literature-review plan.

## Problem statement

Market forecasts describe expected destinations for investment, production, employment, or demand. They do not establish that local small firms possess the documentation systems and qualification evidence required to enter the modeled supply chains.

ORB's working hypothesis is that documentation and qualification burden can be a binding constraint on small-firm participation even when a firm has relevant technical capability. The study will examine that hypothesis through observable requirements, evidence readiness, process controls, and qualification gaps. No result is claimed before fieldwork.

The existence of a practical problem does **not** establish a scholarly research gap. Before ORB finalizes its assessment instruments or claims novelty, it will review established literature on rework causation, submittal/RFI processing, construction defect taxonomy, requirements volatility, and small-firm regulatory/compliance burden to determine which mechanisms and measures already exist.

## Related research mechanism

The companion `federal-submittal-compliance-model` repository examines contractor-side obligation compliance in federal construction. ORB examines supplier-side qualification burden in advanced manufacturing. The two studies address a related mechanism in different populations: organizations must convert external requirements into controlled, traceable, and renewable evidence.

The relationship is conceptual. No cross-population equivalence, causal transfer, or common validated instrument has yet been established.

## Current verified findings

The NASA Office of Small Business Programs confirmed in writing in 2026 that NASA had not published outcome data specific to the Small Business Supplier Development Program. The office identified the SAM.gov Data Bank and USAspending.gov as available sources for broader participation data (NASA Office of Small Business Programs, 2026a).

The office also confirmed that only a small number of several thousand NASA prime contractors and subcontractors participated in the program and that a revitalization initiative was intended to expand participation (NASA Office of Small Business Programs, 2026b). The source supports no exact participant count.

The City of New Orleans Office of Economic Development confirmed a corrected pipeline table in September 2026. The table reports $630,500,000 in currently identified capital expenditure across 1,645 jobs, replacing a previously printed total of $470,660,000 (City of New Orleans Office of Economic Development, 2026). Several pipeline values remained undetermined, so the corrected capital figure is not a final buildout ceiling.

These are bounded source-verification findings. They are not results from ORB fieldwork and do not establish the proposed research gap.

## Research sequence

The current sequence is:

1. **Literature-gap review** — determine what is already established and whether a meaningful unresolved gap remains.
2. **Unit-of-analysis and construct definition** — confirm which constructs and analytical units are defensible for the supplier-readiness study.
3. **Instrument refinement** — revise the proposed domains, codebook, and measures using established literature first.
4. **Pilot and validation** — test burden, scoring clarity, reliability, and evidence rules before formal fieldwork.
5. **Field study** — proceed only with appropriate authority, consent, publication controls, and feasible sampling.

See [`docs/literature-review-plan.md`](docs/literature-review-plan.md) and [`docs/research-design.md`](docs/research-design.md).

## Scope boundaries

- The project studies readiness and documentation requirements. It does not certify suppliers.
- The project does not place firms into federal or prime-contractor programs.
- The repository does not claim a partnership, sponsorship, official affiliation, or government endorsement.
- The study has no site control and makes no development commitment.
- The research gap and novelty are not yet established.
- Public files exclude named individuals, private correspondence, budgets, staffing plans, and speculative identities for coded development projects.
